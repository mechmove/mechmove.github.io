Winter Wolves

A fable for the children of a winter that never ended

There are stories that families carry without ever telling, memories that survive in fragments — a scent, a shadow, a silence at the dinner table. In every generation, someone eventually looks back and realizes the past was not simply “hard,” but shaped by a winter so deep it changed the way everyone learned to walk.

This is a mythic retelling of that winter.

Two young wolves, not yet grown, found themselves leading a pack through a season of cold that swallowed whole valleys. They were caretakers before they were adults, guardians before they understood danger, survivors before they understood loss. The younger wolves behind them lived because they kept moving.

The winter ended, but its echo did not.

Some wolves grew steady. Some fractured. Some learned to see danger everywhere. Some learned to see opportunity. And some — the ones who came later — inherited stories without context, emotions without origins, and expectations shaped by a season they never lived through.

This fable is not about wolves. It is about the quiet architecture of family, the weight of survival, and the way trauma travels across generations without ever announcing itself.

It is a story for anyone who has ever wondered why people behave the way they do — and why some winters never fully thaw.

Some posts will be technical, some personal, some reflective, some about music, some about the world around me. I’m not aiming for perfection or polish. I’m aiming for honesty.

    Life is a mix of routine, surprises, small joys, and the occasional rough moment. I’m still learning, still adjusting, still paying attention. This space is where I capture that — the real stuff, the daily stuff, the things worth remembering.
