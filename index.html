    • Tom’s Absurd Universe

    This series is a playful, curious, and gently self‑aware tour through a universe where physics has a personality, cosmic forces file paperwork, and the laws of nature occasionally lodge formal complaints. What begins as a lighthearted cast of characters—Photons, Entropy, Dark Energy, Space, Mass—slowly evolves into a more reflective exploration of how the universe might work and what it means to live inside it.

    Along the way, the episodes weave together humor, metaphor, and genuine scientific admiration. Einstein, Bohr, and Feynman appear not as authorities to challenge, but as companions in curiosity. The interludes offer comic relief from the deeper questions, while the later episodes reveal the author’s own evolving thoughts about tensors, time, responsibility, and the strange beauty of a probabilistic world.

    This is not a theory. It’s not a claim. It’s a living trail of thought—open, revisable, and intentionally humble. A place where physics becomes a conversation, not a conclusion.

    The series pauses at a natural plateau, not because the journey is over, but because this is a good place to look around, catch your breath, and enjoy the view before wandering further.
